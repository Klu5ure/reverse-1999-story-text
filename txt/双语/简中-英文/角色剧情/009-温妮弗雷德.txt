01  旧屋遗梦 / An Old Dream Left

To Winnifred
致温妮弗雷德

An Old Dream Left
旧屋遗梦

My name is Brian Finn.
我的名字是布莱恩·芬努。

A ... less than accomplished film director.
一位……拙劣的电影导演。

With the meager talents given to me by God, I've made films that defy classification as either good or bad.
凭着上帝所恩宠的微薄天赋，我拍下了一部部谈不上好坏的电影。

Yet, I've never managed to create a film I can truly call my own.
但始终没能完成一部真正的，属于我的影片。

Now, even the generosity of the divine has reached its end.
现在，慷慨的上帝也终于要收回这一切了。

Bedroom
卧室

Brian: *cough*
布莱恩：咳、咳咳……

His rough hand trembles as it gropes around.
粗糙的手颤颤巍巍地摸索着。

It runs over the table's edge, brushes past a half-open medicine box, knocks over an uncapped pen, but never reaches its intended destination.
它攀上了桌沿，扫过半开的药盒，撞倒尚未合起的钢笔，但始终没能抵达它的终点。

Illia: Dad, stop looking through those old film reels. Dr. David said you should be resting in bed!
伊利亚：父亲，您别再去翻那些胶卷了。大卫医生说过，您现在应该在床上静养！

Illia: Besides, your work's already won over all those investors, and they're sharper than Wall Street's finest.
伊利亚：更何况，您的作品早就得到了那些投资商们的认可。要知道，他们可是比华尔街上的苍蝇们还要精明……

Illia: What regrets could you possibly have left?
伊利亚：您还有什么可遗憾的呢？

Her brother speaks up, his pride in his father's achievements ringing in his voice.
另一旁的男孩也开了口，他为自己父亲的成就而骄傲。

Robin: Yeah, Dad, the critics have nothing but praise for your movies, especially New England Dream!
罗宾：是啊，父亲，外面的影评人都对您的那些电影赞不绝口，尤其是那部《新英格兰之梦》！

The old man's hand lingers in the air.
老人的手迟迟没有垂下。

Brian: No, my dear boy. New England Dream is the biggest regret of my life.
布莱恩：不，我亲爱的孩子们……《新英格兰之梦》是我一生的遗憾。

Brian: Forty years squandered on that thing, and I still never truly completed it.
布莱恩：我虚耗了四十余年，却始终没能真正地完成它。

Brian: The editing alone took up years of my life.
布莱恩：光是剪辑其中的片段与镜头，便足足耗去了我好几年的时间——

Brian: But no matter how many changes I made, I couldn't fill that emptiness. That failure has haunted me for decades. To this very day, in fact.
布莱恩：可是，无论我怎么修改，都无法弥补其中的缺口……这个缺憾伴随了我数十年，直到今天。

Illia: ...
伊利亚：……

Brian: At this point, I have no choice but to confess that I failed to capture the essence of a true vampire.
布莱恩：事到如今，我不得不承认，我没能拍出真正的吸血鬼……

Brian: Not even ... Not even a sliver of that elusive inspiration.
布莱恩：甚至、甚至我无法抓住那灵感的一星半点——

The old man becomes agitated, sparking a coughing fit.
老人的情绪激动起来，咳嗽声持续了许久。

Brian: Compared to the vampire I saw, it lacks something vital, some essential element.
布莱恩：相比起真正的、我所睹见的吸血鬼，它缺少了一样东西，一样最为重要的东西。

Robin: But what could that be, Dad?
罗宾：可……它是什么呢？父亲。

Brian: *cough* I ... I don't know.
布莱恩：咳咳、我……我不知道。

A hushed silence falls in the bedroom. Even the sound of Brian's hurried breaths is fading.
卧房内静悄悄的，连急促的呼吸声也渐渐消失了。

They are becoming slow and intermittent, as if they might cease at any moment.
它变得缓慢，时断时续，似乎随时就要熄灭。

A profound regret weighs heavily upon each breath.
某种沉重的遗憾死死地压住了它。

Brian: ...
布莱恩：……

Brian: Son, would you get me my notebook from the desk?
布莱恩：亲爱的，你能帮我把桌上的本子拿过来吗？

Brian: Yes, and turn it to the latest page.
布莱恩：是的，翻到最新的那一页。

Robin: Yes, Dad. I'll get it right away.
罗宾：我这就帮您拿过来。

The boy hastens out the door, while the girl looks at her father with a melancholy gaze, her face etched with concern.
男孩快步出了房门，而女孩忧郁地凝望着父亲，面露愁容。

Illia: But your eyes ...
伊利亚：可您的眼睛……

Brian: Heh. Don't worry about me, Illia.
布莱恩：呵呵……别为我担心，伊利亚～

His eyes have long lost their vitality. They are embedded in deep, wrinkled sockets.
他的瞳孔早已没有生气，直楞楞地嵌在盘根错节的皱纹里。

Brian: It's been my faithful companion for many years. Even with these failing eyes of mine, I can still read the thing through touch alone.
布莱恩：它可是我多年的老朋友了……哪怕不用眼睛去看，我也能摸出它的每一行纹理。

Robin: Dad, here. Here it is.
罗宾：父亲，喏……它在这儿。

Robin carefully places the notebook in his father's hand.
笔记本被小心翼翼地塞进了手里。

Brian: Ah, yes, this is far more useful than any concoction of pills. Don't you agree, old friend?
布莱恩：这可比那些瓶瓶罐罐的药丸要有用得多……你说是吧，我的老友。

With some effort, he steadies his frail frame.
双手费力地撑起了摇摇欲坠的身体。

Brian: I still need you to document everything faithfully. At least for now, anyway.
布莱恩：至少现在，你还得帮我把这一切都忠实地记录下来。

Brian: We have one last journey to make—the final stretch.
布莱恩：我们还有一段路要走……最后的一段路。

Brian: *cough" Now, where should I begin?
布莱恩：咳……该从哪儿写起呢？

Brian: This tale begins in New England, I believe.
布莱恩：我想，故事的起点应该在新英格兰。

Brian: Back then, I was young, timid, and filled with fear of the unknown.
布莱恩：那时候的我尚且年轻，怯懦，对未知的一切都充满恐惧。

1930s, New England
30年代，新英格兰

A cabin, nestled between dense woods and a lake. A common sight in any horror film.
木屋被慎重地藏在密林与湖泊之间，如所有恐怖故事所滥用的那样。

Moss climbs the windowsills and eaves of the cabin's exterior, as if silently repelling any visitors.
青苔爬满了木屋外的窗沿与飞檐，似乎在无声地拒绝所有的来客。

Sadly, it does nothing to repel young, reckless children.
令人遗憾的是，它也理所当然地吸引着那些年轻的、荷尔蒙分泌过量的孩子们。

Tom: D**n! You reckon there's really a witch here brewing potions from baby hair?
汤姆：该死的，这儿真的会有拿婴儿体毛调配魔鬼药剂的女巫吗？

Jared: Haha! What kind of baloney are you talking about now?
杰瑞德：哈哈～你在胡说些什么呢？

Jared: I heard there's a vampire hanging around in these woods. You know, the kind that has fangs with hollow tips so it can pierce the skin on your neck and suck your blood.
杰瑞德：我听说的可是这片林子里藏着一位吸食人血的吸血鬼，他长着细长的獠牙，尖端的空洞锋利，能够轻易地划破你的皮肤，割断你的喉咙——

He places his hand on the quivering shoulder beside him.
他搭上了身旁正瑟瑟发抖的那副纤弱的肩膀。

Jared: Good grief! Would you look at our Little Brian? If he shivered any faster, he could be a hand-crank operator at my uncle's generator factory!
杰瑞德：天哪！瞧瞧我们的小布莱恩，如果你发抖的速度再快一点儿，我肯定愿意向舅舅推荐你进入手摇发电机的试验工厂。

Tom: I bet he's already got a fresh diaper at the ready!
汤姆：我敢打赌，杰瑞德，他肯定早就准备好了新的尿布。

Brian: C-Cut it out, guys.
布莱恩：……别、别开玩笑了。

Brian: We should head back. I really don't want to fall behind on Mrs. Madison's homework.
布莱恩：我们该回去了，我可不想拖欠麦迪逊太太布置的作业……

Jared pats him on the shoulder and pushes him forward.
杰瑞德友好地拍了拍他的肩膀，不容置疑地将他推着向前。

Jared: Don't worry, Little Brian. Legend has it that the vampire's keen on blood from strapping young men, not scrawny little monkeys!
杰瑞德：别担心，小布莱恩。传说告诉我们，那位吸血鬼先生热衷于年轻、健壮的鲜血，他对瘦皮猴可半点兴趣都没有～

Tom: Hah. Then Brian will be the safest one among us!
汤姆：唔，看起来小布莱恩是我们这儿最安全的人了。

The pressure on Brian's shoulder intensifies.
肩膀上的力度越发大了起来。

Brian: That l-legend's probably a bunch of hooey, right?
布莱恩：传说、传说很可能是假的，不是么？

Tom: Hahaha! Of course it is!
汤姆：哈哈哈，当然，当然！

Jared: Yeah, so what are you so afraid of, Little Brian?
杰瑞德：那你还害怕些什么呢？我们勇敢的小布莱恩同学！

A breeze blows past the door, causing it to creak creepily.
一阵阴恻恻的风从门外刮来，门被撞得吱呀作响。

Tom: Off you go, good sir! Take a gander deeper inside for us and say, "How do you do?" to Mr. Vampire!
汤姆：去吧，好先生——！替我们去更深处看看，再替我们向那位吸血鬼先生问好～

The shove from behind sends Brian staggering forward.
粗鲁的推力从背后袭来，布莱恩被撞得打了个趔趄。

He falls into an even more sinister-looking room, hitting his head on the corner of a table.
他摔进一间看上去更阴森的房间，脑袋磕上了桌子的一角。

Brian: Ahh!
布莱恩：啊——！

Tom: Oh, look what you've done now, you klutz! You've scared off all the critters!
汤姆：噢，看看你这个冒失鬼，你把这儿的小动物们都吓跑了。

Brian: Ouch ... My head ...
布莱恩：我的脑袋，好疼……

Jared: Bye-bye, Little Brian. We'll be waiting for your triumphant return to town.
杰瑞德：再见，小布莱恩，我们会在镇上等你的好消息。

The door slams shut.
门被重重地关上了。

Brian: Don't leave me here! You can't do this to me. I don't want to be eaten by a vampire!
布莱恩：别丢下我，你们不能这样……我不想被吸血鬼吃掉！

His head spins as his consciousness slips away into darkness.
脑袋昏昏沉沉，意识也似乎逐渐落入黑暗之中。

Brian: Ugh ...
布莱恩：唔……

A loud, rumbling sound approaches from afar.
巨大的震动声由远及近。

Brian: That noise ... It feels like my head's about to explode. Good lord, is that an earthquake?!
布莱恩：好吵的声音，我的脑袋快要炸开了……见鬼，这儿地震了吗？

Blue.
蓝色。

A subtle blue seeps into the room.
细微的蓝色渗了出来。

From under the bed. Through the cracks in the wooden floor.
从床底，从嘎吱作响的木质地板中。

Brian: You are ...?
布莱恩：你……你是？

Brian: Am I hallucinating? Or is this ...
布莱恩：它们是……我的错觉吗？还是说……

Brian: Is there really a vampire living here?!
布莱恩：这儿真的生活着一位吸血鬼……？！

Blue ripples wash over the walls. The desk, the lamp, and the wooden chair are all tinged with its cool hue.
蓝色的水纹漫过了四周的墙壁。书桌，台灯，甚至木椅都被晕染上了一层或深或浅的蓝色。

They pick themselves up and start moving. They dance in the endless blue, eventually surrounding the young boy.
它们在无边的蓝色中徜徉，舞蹈，热情地围靠着某副同样年轻的躯体。

It is as if they have been brought to life.
像是被赋予了新的生命。

???: Hm?
？？？：……嗯？

???: You don't look too well, my uninvited friend.
？？？：不请自来的朋友，你看上去可不太好。

???: Need a hand?
？？？：需要我帮忙吗……？

Brian: I-I ... um ... y-you ...
布莱恩：我、我，你……这……

Brian: Are you a vampire?
布莱恩：你是……吸血鬼吗？

The angel-faced woman seems uninclined to answer.
眼前这位面容姣好的年轻女性似乎并不打算回答任何问题。

???: Hah! Clearly, you're in desperate need of a good rest.
？？？：呵呵～显然，你很需要休息。

The blue has reached his temples, blanketing everything in sight.
蓝色终于抵达了额角，盖住了眼前的一切。

He gives in, sinking into slumber and returning to tranquility.
脑袋放弃了抵抗，它沉入梦乡，归于平和。

Brian: I've never forgotten what I saw that day.
布莱恩：我从未遗忘过那一幕。

Brian: I firmly believe that in that cramped little cabin, I saw a true vampire.
布莱恩：我笃信，在那个狭小而逼仄的小木屋里，我见到了一位真正的吸血鬼。

Brian: She was so beautiful, so captivating, and brimming with a passion for life.
布莱恩：她是那样的美丽，动人，充满活力。

Brian: That was our first encounter.
布莱恩：那是我们的第一次见面。

Brian: After that, I fantasized countless times about meeting her again.
布莱恩：在那之后，我曾幻想过无数次与她再见的画面。

Brian: Until ...
布莱恩：直到……

02  天降救星 / Savior out of the Blue

Savior out of the Blue
天降救星

Brian: The war came, forcing all of us to put aside childish things and grow up—myself included.
布莱恩：战争很快便来了，每一个人都被迫着迅速长大，我也一样。

Brian: It was a cruel and solemn mass farewell.
布莱恩：它是一场残忍而严肃的群体性告别仪式。

Brian: It forced every one of us to say goodbye to our dreams, to the past, and to the world we knew.
布莱恩：逼迫着每一个人告别理想，告别过去，告别自己熟稔的一切。

Brian: Before I knew it, I was conscripted and boarded an army jeep, venturing into foreign lands.
布莱恩：很快，我便应征入伍，坐上军绿色的吉普车，踏上了全然陌生的土地。

1940s, Officers' Club
40年代，军官俱乐部

The atmosphere rises amidst the clinking of glasses, as if everyone should toast to the victory at hand ...
气氛在酒杯的交错之间逐渐高涨，似乎所有人都理应为眼前的胜利而举杯。

... while they still can.
在他们仍能举起酒杯之时。

Officer I: Cheers, gents!
军官Ⅰ：干杯，伙计们！

He drains his glass in one gulp.
他将杯中的酒一饮而尽。

Officer II: To a brighter tomorrow!
军官Ⅱ：为美好的明天！

Officer II: Sergeant Finn, why the hell are ya still clutching that little book of yours? You ain't writing sweet nothings to a dame back home, are ya?!
军官Ⅱ：——布莱恩中士，你怎么还拿着那本小本子？该不会是在给家里的小情人写信吧？！

Brian: ...
布莱恩：……

Officer I: Come on, there's nothing wrong with that! And I can tell you now, Pete, when you get back home, you'll be spinning tales even taller than Brian here!
军官Ⅰ：这没什么不好的，我敢保证，皮特，等你回去，你吹嘘得肯定比布莱恩更夸张。

Officer I: Go ahead and write whatever you want, Brian, and don't forget to mention the part where I led the charge.
军官Ⅰ：大胆去写吧，布莱恩……别忘了提上几句我带头冲锋的场面。

He slaps Brian's shoulder heartily. Brian clutches his notebook.
他用力地拍了拍布莱恩的肩头，后者局促地捏紧了笔记本。

Brian: This isn't a letter; it's my research.
布莱恩：这不是信件，而是……我的研究。

Officer II: Oh, now that's something ya don't hear every day! Lemme have a look!
军官Ⅱ：噢……新鲜玩意儿！——让我瞧瞧！

He strides forward, snatching the notebook from Brian's hands.
他一个健步冲上前来，抢过布莱恩手里的本子。

Brian: Hey! They're just a few rough ideas! It's not finished yet!
布莱恩：嘿！这些都只是不成样子的想法，它们还不完整。

The other man doesn't seem to care.
而对方似乎并不在乎这一无力的辩解。

Officer II: "A compilation of data on mysterious vampires ..."
军官Ⅱ：“关于神秘吸血鬼的资料收集——”

Officer II: Haha, you ain't got a torch for our Miss Julia, have ya?
军官Ⅱ：哈哈，你该不会是暗恋我们的茱莉娅小姐吧！

Officer II: I'll admit, she's got a gentle touch when she's bandaging us up, and the way she draws blood is very light! I almost fell asleep the last time she did it.
军官Ⅱ：我承认，她在为我们包扎时相当温柔，抽血的动作也轻飘飘的，像柔软的棉花，我几乎都要睡着了。

Officer I: Tough luck, Brian. You've got more rivals here than a dog has fleas.
军官Ⅰ：这可不是什么好消息，布莱恩，你的竞争对手人数众多。

Officer II: But I'm sure comparing Miss Julia to a vampire would earn you a sock on the jaw from some of the straighter shooters in this squad.
军官Ⅱ：但我可以肯定，你将美丽的茱莉亚小姐比喻成吸血鬼会遭到其他正直朋友们的抨击……

They look at Brian with pity, as if mourning this lost cause.
他们同情地看着布莱恩，像是为对方深切惋惜。

Brian: This isn't for Julia.
布莱恩：这不是写给茱莉亚的……

Brian shakes his head firmly, a serious expression on his face.
布莱恩坚决地摆了摆头，神情严肃。

Brian: Being called a vampire is hardly insulting! They're symbols of beauty, purity, and everlasting life.
布莱恩：吸血鬼可不是什么贬低人的说法！它象征着美好，纯洁，以及长久的生命……

Officer I: Haha, you say that, but have you ever even laid eyes on a real vampire?
军官Ⅰ：哈哈，你说得这么振振有词，难道你见过真的吸血鬼吗？

Officer II: They're just made-up stories I believed when I was a kid.
军官Ⅱ：那些都是我小时候才会相信的老掉牙的传说故事了……

Officer II: But I'll grant ya that they're pretty handy for spooking the little rascals!
军官Ⅱ：当然，我承认这些东西在恐吓小鬼头上很有效果～

Brian: ...
布莱恩：……

Brian: I've seen a real vampire.
布莱恩：我见过真正的吸血鬼。

They fall into a brief silence.
酒会的一角陷入了短暂的寂静。

Officer I: ...
军官Ⅰ：……

Officer II: ...
军官Ⅱ：……

Then, a burst of raucous laughter.
随后，是更热烈的嘲笑。

Officer II: I gotta hand it to ya, Sergeant Finn; your sense of humor's something else. You've got a real knack for story-telling!
军官Ⅱ：我承认，布莱恩中士，你的幽默细胞与说谎的能力令我敬佩。

Brian: I'm being serious. When I was a kid, I saw her.
布莱恩：我是认真的。在我年纪不大的时候，我见到了她。

Brian: Ever since then, I've been on the hunt for more information about her, even just the slightest sliver.
布莱恩：自那以后，我开始寻找更多有关她的资料，哪怕一丝一毫……

Brian: She was perfect. Everything I could ever imagine. Just like—
布莱恩：她是完美的，拥有我所能想象的一切美好样子，就像——

Officer I: Haha, what are you looking at?
军官Ⅰ：哈哈，你在张望什么呢？

Officer II: Brian, ya don't think there's a vampire lurking somewhere at this shindig, do ya?
军官Ⅱ：布莱恩，你该不会觉得这个酒会上正好藏着一位吸血鬼吧！

Brian scans the room, eagerly seeking to validate his claim.
他环顾四周，急切地为自己的观点寻找佐证。

The club is bustling with people. The heavy conversation and crisscrossing lights make his head spin.

布莱恩：那本笔记本仍旧搁在他的胸前。

布莱恩：新的一页露了出来，优雅的古典笔迹仍沾着未干的墨渍。
