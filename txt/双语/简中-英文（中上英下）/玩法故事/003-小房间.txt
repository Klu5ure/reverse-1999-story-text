01  狗门 / Dog Door

狗门
Dog Door

环城大道
Ring Road

观众Ⅰ：多可惜啊，这多可惜啊——
Admirer I: Sad news indeed. Ah, most unfortunate and disappointing news!

绅士捶胸顿足，倍感惋惜。
Stamping his feet in anger, the gentleman lets out a wail of frustration.

观众Ⅰ：她的新剧目就这样取消了？我足足期待了一个季度，只为了她这出新戏！
Admirer I: They canceled her new show, just like that? I've been waiting for it all season!

观众Ⅱ：不取消又能如何呢？难道叫那样一个人上台去为您歌唱吗？
Admirer II: What else could be done? Surely you wouldn't expect her to sing after what happened?

观众Ⅱ：希望您多一些同情心，这对她太过于残忍了……
Admirer II: Have some compassion for her, will you? Fate has dealt her a cruel hand in life ...

随行女伴口中的指责，轻柔如同丝绒，不足以构成真正的威胁。
Although this lady immediately volleys criticism, there is nothing accusatory in the softness of her tone.

观众Ⅲ：我们的维也纳失去了一颗闪亮的明星，至少在这个演出季如此。
Admirer III: Vienna has lost its star, at least for this season.

另一个男人加入对谈，他目光暗沉，两颗眼睛如同河堤下淤泥包裹着的灰色石头。
Another man joins the conversation. Also terribly hit by the news, his eyes are as dim and cold as two lifeless cobblestones at the bottom of a river.

他走在失落的观众们的最后，步履拖沓。
He walks at the rear of the crowd with heavy footsteps.

观众Ⅲ：我……都看见了，整个始末，我都瞧见了。
Admirer III: I ... saw it with my own eyes. The entire thing.

观众Ⅲ：她从维也纳剧院出来，打算去威格尔德雷尔公园散步，就像是往常周四下午她常做的那样。
Admirer III: She left the Vienna State Opera in the afternoon. She must have planned to take a walk in Weigl's Dreher Park, as she always does on Thursday afternoons.

观众Ⅲ：那时候她……她漆黑的卷发披散着，其中裹着的脸惨白得吓人。
Admirer III: On that day, her dark, curly hair cascaded over her shoulders, and her face was pale as porcelain.

观众Ⅲ：她穿着一件薰衣草紫的长裙，手上披着软毛编制的薄巾，裹在其中的身子比玻璃杯壁还单薄。
Admirer III: She was in a lavender dress with a soft wool shawl around her arms. She looked pretty and fragile, like a doll made of glass.

观众Ⅱ：你是说，你看见了她……
Admirer II: Did you see her—

观众Ⅲ：是的，我瞧见了她晕倒的时候。
Admirer III: Yes, I saw her faint.

观众Ⅲ：她告别了剧院的管理人，那时她还正常，可不出几步，她就忽然颤抖起来……
Admirer III: She seemed perfectly fine when she took leave of the theater manager. But after a few steps, she started shaking ...

观众Ⅲ：她一面呜咽一面用双手抓挠自己的脸颊，像是一只落水的疯猫那样嘶嘶尖叫着——
Admirer III: She whimpered and scratched at her pretty face. Her usual heavenly voice became the howling cry of a mad cat.

他喃喃的声音提高，眼中也见了光彩。
He murmurs with a dark heat in his tone. A light of sinister delight flickers in his eyes.

观众Ⅲ：她的随行女仆想要拦住她，但一切都太迟了，她想奔跑，却被裙摆绊倒，从……从……从阿尔贝蒂娜台阶上跌落了下来！立刻晕死了过去——！
Admirer III: Her servants tried to stop her, but it was too late! She ran off, tripped over her dress, and—and fell down the Albertina stairs! They helped her up, but by then, she'd already fainted.

观众Ⅱ：哦！天啊！那可真可怕！
Admirer II: God in Heaven! Poor thing!

受到惊吓的听众们小声尖叫起来。
His audience responds with gasps and sobs.

观众Ⅲ：是，很快，很快……她的医生来了，他本就在不远处，只是暂时走开。
Admirer III: It breaks one's heart to see such a thing. Soon, her physician came. He'd been by her side all day. It was only in the brief moment in which that horrid affair occurred that he was away attending to another matter.

观众Ⅱ：他带走了她？去了医院吗？她还好吗？
Admirer II: Did he take her to the hospital? How is she now?

观众Ⅱ：哦，天啊，她……实在是个可怜的人……
Admirer II: Oh, heavens, this is terribly sad. Most unfortunate for a talented lady such as her ...

观众Ⅱ：她的身体本就那样柔弱，如同一阵烟雾、一枚圆滑的肥皂泡，无需棍棒针尖，只需要一阵呵斥般的风，就足以令她昏厥，更何况是……是……
Admirer II: She's so delicate. Even a strong gust of wind could whisk her off her feet. And now she has to come to terms with the painful fact ...

观众Ⅱ：是失去孩子这样痛苦的遭遇……
Admirer II: ... that her dear child has been cruelly taken from her ...

富有同情心的女士打开母贝制的折扇，遮掩住自己被泪水浸透的双颊。
The lady whips open her mother-of-pearl fan to hide the tears that are now streaming down her face.

观众Ⅱ：我只感谢上帝为她留下了西奥菲尔，可她显然并没有因此得到安慰……
Admirer II: Thank goodness, she still has Theophil. Still, it does little to ease a mother's pain ...

她喃喃着，注视如同珠宝一般佩戴在她的额前与项上。
She continues to mutter to herself. Though her cheeks are still wet with the tears of compassion, there is a quiet joy in her heart as she attracts the others' gaze.

观众Ⅱ：也是，也是，是谁都会无法面对的……
Admirer II: None of us—none of us would ever have the courage to face such tragedy ...

观众Ⅱ：就在三日前的降灵会上，我与莉莉玛莲与菲欧娜都所在的降灵会上，那个孩子就死在我眼前……她的女儿，第一个可爱的小女儿。
Admirer II: Having to witness her own child taken by death at the seance three days ago must have destroyed her ... We were all there, you know, Lili Marlene, Fiona and I. That poor girl. Such a lovely little thing.

观众Ⅱ：是可怜的小翠斯特，她才只有三岁呀……
Admirer II: Little Trista. She was only three years old ...

观众Ⅱ：那张红润的小脸儿不到一分钟就变成了灰败的蓝色，苍白的口唇中有血，眼中淌着金色的泪，她就……就这样死了。
Admirer II: Her rosy cheeks turned a grayish-blue within seconds. Her pale lips were stained red with blood, and two golden tears welled in her empty eyes and ran down her cheeks. She died in a moment ... just like that.

观众Ⅱ：可那一首像是小鸟儿一样吱吱喳喳的歌儿，足足在她喉咙里装了一整夜……
Admirer II: But that sweet song didn't fade away. For the whole night, it was trapped in her throat, like a little songbird captive in a cage ...

观众Ⅱ：直到第二天清晨，人们才敢去抚摸她的身体，她那时候还是柔软的，就像……就像活着时一样……
Admirer II: It wasn't until morning that we mustered enough strength to touch her. She was still soft ... as if she were only sleeping ...

女士的扇子扑扑扇动，随着那未曾流传的细节被揭露，人们纷纷屏息感叹。
She gently flutters her fan as she reveals more and more details to the crowd. They listen in breathless suspense.

观众Ⅰ：我从未……听过这些细节。
Admirer I: I ... I didn't know that.

观众Ⅰ：好吧，好吧，是我苛责了她——遭遇这样的惨剧，任何人都没有精神再登台歌唱了！
Admirer I: Fine, I admit it! I was too harsh on her. No one would be able to sing on stage in the wake of such misfortune!

观众Ⅰ：她的休息是理所应当的，孩子，为了孩子，这多令人心碎……
Admirer I: She should rest. She must be devastated, poor thing, losing her child like that.

绅士掏出胸口的丝巾，擦过湿润的眼睑，适当地表现出足够的爱心。
The gentleman takes out his silk handkerchief and dabs at the corner of his moistened eyes. This is a situation in which sympathy is the only appropriate response.

队伍末尾又传来声音，阻断了人们的结论。
But a voice from the other side of the crowd breaks the sad silence.

观众Ⅲ：不，她不是因为那个死去的女儿放弃表演的！
Admirer III: No, she didn't cancel the performance to mourn her daughter!

观众Ⅲ：她在女儿去世之后仍旧前往排演，你们忘了吗？我昨天还瞧见了她！
Admirer III: Did you forget? She showed up at the rehearsals after her daughter's death. I saw her just yesterday!

他对人们的判决感到不满，面色涨红。
His face reddens with frustration at these speculations.

观众Ⅲ：她的医生在当晚照旧来找了我，他用双手捧着一只剖开的兔子，告诉了我她的身体状况！她——
Admirer III: Her doctor came to me that very night with a dissected rabbit in his palms. A quick look made it very clear.

那两颗石子震颤着，像是兔子腔内两枚鼓胀、湿润又赤红的卵巢。
His eyes are red and bulging, just like the pair of distended ovaries he saw inside the rabbit.

观众Ⅲ：她怀孕了！时间已经有了两个月！在她的女儿离开后的三天，她又有了一个新的孩子！
Admirer III: She is with child again—two months into her pregnancy, in fact! The announcement of a new child just three days after her daughter's tragic death!

观众Ⅲ：那只兔子的变化证明了一切，天啊，为什么我不能给你们瞧瞧它呢？那是无可辩驳的真相！
Admirer III: The change in the rabbit's ovaries told us everything. I wish I could show you, good people, for it is evidence most irrefutable!

观众Ⅲ：伊文洁琳必须去休息，她别无选择！无论她多么热爱我们，她都别无选择！
Admirer III: Evangeline must rest, no matter how much she loves the stage and her audience. She has no choice!

观众Ⅲ：她——别无——选择！
Admirer III: She. Has. No. Choice!

？？？：是的，这就是她第一次出现在维也纳人们口中时的场景。
???: That was her first appearance in Vienna.

？？？：一个糟糕的跟踪狂，买通了母亲的家庭医生，他用一种尚不为人知的、有些惊世骇俗的、温热而又毛茸茸的验孕方法证实了她的生命。
???: A fanatic admirer bribed her mother's doctor, subsequently discovering her existence through a fluffy—and oddly bloody—pregnancy test.

？？？：也许再过一段时间，用兔子和小老鼠来验证女人是否怀孕会成为一种主流呢？
???: Perhaps soon these rabbit tests will become a mainstream method of determining pregnancy.

？？？：哈哈……在这个世界上，谁也说不好。
???: Haha ... Who knows what will happen in this world of turmoil?

衣物摩擦的声响从你后方靠近，那是一个矮小的身影，一个孩子。
Someone approaches from behind. The figure of a child.

？？？：无与伦比的戏剧性。她是天生的明星。
???: Favored by the god of theater, this child is a star—even from before birth.

？？？：以至于多年后她的首秀演出之前，人们谈论起这个即将面试的女演员时，总会以“踏着姐姐的死亡出生的姑娘”来形容她。
???: In the many years that followed, before she made her debut, people talked of her as "the girl who arrived upon the wings of death."

？？？：但她真的是一个演员吗？
???: But is she really an opera singer?

她声响停顿，像是在等待着答案。
The voice pauses, awaiting an answer.

？？？：我是说，你瞧她，伊索尔德，我可爱的小妹妹，她在维也纳闻名遐迩，叫好又叫座。
???: Look at her. Isolde, my lovely little sister. She's the new star of Vienna, gaining both fame and fortune in the theater.

？？？：她作为演员的声名已经吞没了我们著名的母亲，让她的功绩只剩下了骇人听闻的谋杀。
???: As a singer, her name has surpassed that of our mother, who is now only remembered as the perpetrator of that appalling crime.

？？？：可她从没有真正地演出过一场剧目，哪怕一场。
???: But my sister has never performed in a play. Not even once.

？？？：就像我如今站在她的身体之上，我的声音游荡在她喉咙里。我的表情浮现在她面容上……
???: She's there, but she also isn't. She's many people, including me—my voice in her throat, my smile on her face …

？？？：不只是我，还有许多，游灵们在这里徘徊，轮番登场，去而又返。
???: Upon the stage, many spirits wander.

？？？：男人的声音，女人的声音，孩子的声音都在这里——不同的姿态与面孔交替，歌声如泣如诉。那是我们，那不是她。
???: There are men's voices, women's voices, even children's—you can find them all. Different songs, different stories ... But they aren't hers.

？？？：是啊，是啊……将降灵术与歌剧表演融为一体，这是一例创新的壮举……
???: Yes, yes ... rather innovative, isn't it? To channel spirits in an operatic performance.

？？？：那些满溢的情绪和真心从她的眼与口中流淌而出，她托着游灵，就像舞台坚实地承托着她的双脚……
???: Emotions she has never felt gleam in her eyes and flow from her mouth. She holds the spirits as they gently fall upon her, just as she is held by the stage.

？？？：是啦，我亲爱的傻瓜——我的小妹妹从来都不是台上的人，她是舞台上的舞台，一盏漂亮的玻璃樽！
???: Yes, silly goose. My little sister was never an actress. She's but a stage upon a stage—a pretty glass vessel!

一只小小的手搭在肩头，轻柔地前推，指引出方向。
A little hand softly touches your shoulder, and the other points into the darkness.

？？？：来，看那。
???: Now, look over there.

???: A door? Hmm, why's there a door? How strange!

？？？：……你一定是这么想的，我能猜到。
???: Am I right? Did I guess what you were thinking?

模糊的人影发出一阵笑声。
The figure bursts into delighted laughter.

???: We all know what a stage looks like. There are no walls, but there are doors.

？？？：不，不是剧场供观众进入的门，那是舞台之下的门，我们在谈论的是舞台之上的门，即使存在也被遮掩的门——通往后台的门。
???: No, not the entrance doors to the theater. I'm talking about the doors behind the wings—the hidden ones that lead backstage.

“叩叩。”门扉经由手扣响。
The hand raps on the door.

？？？：这是第一扇门。
???: This is the first door.

???: A narrow doggie door sitting an inch above the floor, painted in a calming blue. Who uses it, I wonder?

？？？：是一只伯德梗吗？它重六千克，不胖也不瘦，恰好可以从此进入。
???: Could it be a Border Terrier? At a well-proportioned 6 kilograms, it would fit through the door perfectly.

小梗犬们相互追逐着远去了，一阵旋风拂过你的小腿左侧。
The terriers sprint past you as they chase one another, a flurry of wind rushing by your left calf.

更高、更重、更加稳重的生物从身后的黑暗中走来，它的呼吸温热，正在右侧大腿下半吹拂。
Something taller, heavier, and calmer emerges from the dark. A warm breath settles on the skin of your right calf.

宅邸卧室
Bedroom

伊文洁琳：我的宝贝，别哭泣，你正在摇篮中安睡……
Evangeline: Hush, my baby, dry your tears; the cradle's safe and warm ...

翠斯特：啊，是伊文洁琳。我们的妈咪，我们曾经的居所。温和的歌声的来源。
Trista: Ah, Evangeline is here. The source of the sweet humming. Our mama. We all once lived inside her.

翠斯特：她习惯用左手扶着摇篮，那里头曾盛放着西奥菲尔、我，再来是我的小妹妹伊索尔德。
Trista: She always rocked this cradle with her left hand. It's the same one that Theophil, I, and our little sister, Isolde, all used.

翠斯特：……
Trista: ...

翠斯特：她在为小妹妹唱歌，一首摇篮曲。
Trista: She's singing her a lullaby.

Evangeline: You always get dizzy under the sun. How can you attend your lessons if you're unwell?

伊文洁琳：现在我们来到客厅，也可以透过窗户见到阳光，也可以欣赏音乐，这只会让放松更彻底，也更完美。
Evangeline: The lounge is much better. We can enjoy a bit of sunshine through the windows and listen to music.

母亲拍了拍身边的软垫，伊索尔德顺从地坐下，从她怀中接过了苹果卷——一只嫩黄色的小兔子。
Her mother pats the cushion next to her. Isolde sits on it, taking Apfelstrudel, the pale yellow rabbit, into her arms.

伊索尔德：啊，小苹果卷……你还好吗？
Isolde: Hello, little Apfelstrudel ... How're you feeling?

伊索尔德：让我摸摸你，嗯，你摸起来还是这么柔软，你的确应该叫这个名字啦……
Isolde: You're so fluffy and soft! Like the cooked apple in a real strudel ...

小姑娘抱着小兔子，轻轻地抚摸它，摇晃它，看向它的眼睛。
She holds the rabbit, stroking its fur and looking into its eyes.

伊文洁琳：它的毛发总是最柔软的，颜色也可爱。
Evangeline: She has the softest fur—and such adorable coloring.

伊文洁琳：你还记得你父亲第一次带回它时的场景吗？
那时候你才十一岁，比现在还矮小几厘米，刚刚开始上声乐课。
Evangeline: Do you remember when your father first brought them home?
You were eleven—a bit shorter than you are now. It wasn't long after you started your vocal lessons.

伊索尔德：我记得，妈咪。苹果卷和花儿是我们家第一对儿小兔子。
Isolde: Of course, Mama. Apfelstrudel and Blume are the only rabbits who've joined the family.

伊索尔德：虽然从前我们也养着观赏鸟儿与小马驹，也有用于降灵仪式的白兔，可它们从来都没有名字。
Isolde: We've had plenty of birds and ponies before, as well as white rabbits for use in the seances, but none of them ever had a name.

伊索尔德：医生建议您更换疗法，所以父亲带回了它们，我们也遵循医嘱，第一次为它们取了名字……
Isolde: It wasn't until the doctor recommended a new method of therapy that Father brought these two home, and we named them.

伊文洁琳：呵呵，是啊。多么特殊，多么有趣儿。
Evangeline: *chuckles* Yes. It was fun.

伊文洁琳：在这些小家伙的陪伴下，我的晕厥次数变少了不少，而你也成为了一个十五岁的大孩子，唱歌儿唱得这样好……
Evangeline: With these adorable little things around, I don't faint as often as I used to. You were so small back then. I can scarcely believe that that little girl is the same person as the fifteen-year-old young lady sitting before me. And for her to be so beautiful a singer, too!

阳光笼罩着母亲。她依旧微笑着，手下一次又一次抚摸着兔子柔软的皮毛。
The sun shines through the window, enveloping Evangeline. With a peaceful smile on her face, she continues to stroke the rabbit in her arms.

伊索尔德：妈咪……
Isolde: Mama ...

伊索尔德：您真的不需要回到舞台上吗？您一直都很喜欢唱歌的……
Isolde: Do you not wish to go back on stage? Singing used to be so important to you ...

伊文洁琳：……不。
Evangeline: ... No.

伊文洁琳依旧微笑着。
She maintains the smile on her face.

伊文洁琳：不，我的孩子，不。我并没有十分喜爱歌剧。那只是我的一部分罢了。
Evangeline: You're mistaken, my child. I never really loved performing. It was just an ordinary part of my life.

伊文洁琳：它们让我情绪激动，从而引发病症的加剧，我不应该再继续登上舞台了。那对我的身体不好。
Evangeline: Besides, they were making me emotional, which worsened my condition. I shouldn't be performing anymore. It's no good for me.

伊索尔德：可是……
Isolde: But ...

伊文洁琳：不，我没事儿。就像是医生说的，照顾的善举也会带来灵魂的安宁。
Evangeline: I'll be fine. As the doctor said, caring for animals brings me peace.

伊文洁琳：多多与温顺的动物接触，心情也会变得平和。一颗平和的心是最为重要的，是这疗法重点中的重点……伊索尔德，我的孩子，我们得坚持下去。
Evangeline: Keeping a calm atmosphere is the foremost goal of this treatment ... Isolde, my child, we must carry on.

在希冀的目光中，女儿低下头，顺从地贴近母亲。
Beneath her mother's hopeful gaze, Isolde lowers her head and meekly snuggles up to her.

伊索尔德：是的，妈咪。
……我们会坚持下去的。
Isolde: Yes, Mama.
We must carry on.

伊文洁琳：不过你不一样，伊索尔德，你很幸运。你接触到治疗的时间更早，你遇到的时代更好，你所可以选择的治疗方式更多，也更有效……
Evangeline: Things will be different for you, Isolde. You're luckier than I. You've received treatment much earlier in life, and times are better now. There's more than one way to improve your situation ...

伊文洁琳：你是可以痊愈的，你也具有唱歌的天赋。
Evangeline: You'll be cured. And you have the talent to sing.

母亲的声音从上方传来，却遥远得如同在云层那端。
Her mother's voice seems to come from above her head. For no clear reason, Isolde feels as though she is speaking from somewhere even higher.

伊文洁琳：你会成为维也纳首屈一指的歌剧女演员，你要继续唱下去。
Evangeline: You will become the star of Vienna. You must keep on.

伊索尔德：是的，妈咪。是的。我会接受治疗，我会继续唱下去。
Isolde: Yes, Mama. I'll continue with the treatment. I'll keep on singing.

伊索尔德：我会……嗯？
Isolde: I'll—Hm?

苹果卷不是一只聪明的兔子，她对母女之间的交流毫不关心，只是专心致志地啃着伊索尔德的手套。
Apfelstrudel has started to nibble on her young master's gloves.

伊索尔德：苹果卷？你……饿了吗？
Isolde: Apfelstrudel? Are you hungry?

伊文洁琳：怎么会呢，今天女仆们应该已经喂过兔子了。
Evangeline: How could she be? The maids have already fed them today.

伊索尔德：不，妈咪，您又忘了，从三月份开始，医生就建议让我们来喂养苹果卷与花儿了。
Isolde: No, you're confused again, Mama. In March, the doctor suggested that you start feeding them.

伊索尔德：他说这样能让它们和我们更亲近，也能加强人与动物的互动。
Isolde: He said it would help you bond with them.

伊文洁琳：是吗……？哦，似乎是有这么一件事儿来着……
Evangeline: Is that so ...? Oh yes, I remember ...

放下未成年的幼兔，伊索尔德为恍惚的母亲拨开散碎的鬓发，她轻轻地将额头贴在母亲的额头上。
Isolde puts down the rabbit and cups her mother's face. She gently pushes her hair aside and softly presses her forehead against her mother's.

伊索尔德：别担心，妈咪。您不需要费力记得，我会喂小兔子们的。都交给我吧。
Isolde: Don't worry, Mama. We all forget things sometimes. Leave it to me.

伊索尔德：它们不会饿太久的，我这就去取饲料来。
Isolde: I'll get them something to eat.

站起身子，伊索尔德快步走到门边，而后她停下，回头望向母亲。
She gets to her feet and makes her way to the other room. But before she leaves, she stops by the door and looks back.

伊索尔德：对了，妈咪，小兔子们的精神已经恢复了吗？前些日子，我注意到它们似乎有些萎靡。
Isolde: Mama, have the rabbits recovered? I noticed they were a little out of spirits earlier.

伊索尔德：我得知道它们的身体情况，以此来决定是否一起拿上药品。
Isolde: If they're still unwell, I'll go get some medicine, too.

伊文洁琳：嗯？有这回事儿吗？
Evangeline: Mm? Were they really?

伊文洁琳托起兔子，它们健康、有力、鼻尖湿润、眼角洁净。
她身旁没有名字的多只白兔正相互追逐着，每一只都活力四射。
Evangeline raises the rabbits in her hands so that their faces are level with hers. Their noses are wet, and their eyes are clear. They look as healthy as any rabbit she has seen. A few nameless white rabbits are chasing each other around her feet, each looking as energetic as ever.

伊文洁琳：啊……我想，至少它们现在没事儿了。
Evangeline: They seem ... fine to me.

伊索尔德：好的，妈咪。我很快就回来。
Isolde: Alright Mama. I'll be back shortly.

宅邸前厅
Front Lobby

穿过会客厅，来到另一个会客厅。我们总会赞叹迪塔斯多夫家宅邸的宏伟。
Out of the lounge and through the hall, past the many doorways, down the stairs, and finally to a small room with two maids working inside. The Dittarsdorf house is well-known for its staggering size.

女仆Ⅰ：床单，又是床单……天啊，我们每天都有新的床单要洗——！
Maid I: Sheets, sheets, and more sheets—I ain't gone a single day without washing these bloody things!

女仆Ⅰ：我过去在迈娅家工作时，他们至少要三到五天才有一次共同的洗衣日呢！
Maid I: When I worked for the Mayers, we only had one or two washing days a week!

女仆Ⅱ：呵，迈娅家给到的薪水可和这儿不一样，你还是少些抱怨吧……嗯？
Maid II: But you're paid better here, ain't ya? Quit your complainin' and come help, eh?

女仆Ⅱ：哎呀……又是血，到底怎么样才会在床单上留下这样多的血呢？哪怕是月事，这也太过于夸张了……
Maid II: My God, there's blood on the sheets again. How on Earth is there so much of it? Even for that time of the month, this is a lot ...

女仆Ⅰ：这当然不是月事啦。
Maid I: There's no way this is blood from her time of the month.

姜红色头发的女仆皱起鼻子，像是小狗一样地打了个喷嚏。
The red-haired maid crinkles up her nose in disgust.

女仆Ⅰ：哪有月事会在一个月之中连续三周每周发作一次呢？
Maid I: There's been blood on the sheets once a week for the last three weeks now.

女仆Ⅰ：要我说，这些血八成都是那些死兔子和死鸟的！
Maid I: I'd say it comes from those dead rabbits and birds.

女仆Ⅰ：昨天卢卡斯又处理掉了一批尸体……有鸟，也有小马，但大多都是兔子，有黄兔子，也有白兔子。
Maid I: Lucas threw away another load of dead animals yesterday. I saw birds, ponies, but mostly rabbits—the yellow and white ones.

女仆Ⅰ：我都不知道这是第几批苹果卷和花儿了，总之，今天他们又准备了两只新的……
Maid I: I've lost count of the number of Apfelstrudels and Blumes we've had. They got two new ones just today ...

女仆Ⅱ：真可怜，那些小东西到底怎么了，我记得去年更换还没这么频繁，难道是吃的草料出了什么问题吗？
Maid II: Poor things. What happened to 'em? They didn't die nearly so often last year. Is there somethin' wrong with the fodder?

女仆Ⅰ：不，不是自然死亡！怎么会是自然死亡呢！
Maid I: No, it weren't because of the bloody fodder! It weren't any natural cause of death!

女仆将手下的床单塞入水中，压低了声音。
As the maid soaks the sheet in the water, she lowers her voice.

女仆Ⅰ：我听卢卡斯说，那些小家伙死得可惨烈了，有的口吐鲜血，有的脑袋破裂，也有的脖子上被穿了个孔，里面插着一把金钥匙呢！
Maid I: Lucas told me that every one of the animals suffered a miserable death. Some vomited blood; others had their heads cracked open. Most strange of all is that a few of 'em had a hole in their neck with a golden key jammed inside.

女仆Ⅰ：……他们都说，说是伊文洁琳夫人做的。
Maid I: ... He said Lady Evangeline did it.

女仆Ⅱ：真是瞎说，伊文洁琳夫人最是疼爱动物，她总是抱着兔子们……
Maid II: Oh, come off it! Lady Evangeline loves those animals. She's always carryin' those rabbits around in her arms.

女仆Ⅰ：哈，你只是来得晚，没瞧见她病症发作时将手心里的夜莺捏得稀碎的画面。
Maid I: Well you didn't see her crush that poor nightingale with her bare hands when she was having an episode!

女仆Ⅰ：她身上有疾病，伊索尔德小姐也有，这是遗传的，是诅咒。
Maid I: She's ill. So is Lady Isolde. It runs in the family, like a curse.

女仆Ⅰ：是歇斯底里症，医生说，那是因为子宫在病人的体内失序了，茫然地四处游走、碰撞，从而导致疯狂癔症和异常的出血！
Maid I: The doctor said it was hysteria. Somethin' to do with their "wanderin' wombs." Drives 'em mad and makes 'em bleed and all.

女仆Ⅰ：——你看，不正常的血迹，这都对照上了！
Maid I: Which explains these blood stains.

女仆Ⅱ：那……伊索尔德小姐未来也会变成夫人的样子吗？
Maid II: Do you think Lady Isolde will one day lose her mind like her mother?

女仆Ⅰ：你关心这个做什么？
Maid I: Why do you care?

女仆Ⅱ：她在我刚刚到达这里的时候帮助过我……
Maid II: She's been awfully kind to me. She helped me settle in ...

黑色短发的女仆闭上眼睛，她手下攥着一条被呕吐物染污的床单。
The maid with short, dark hair looks down at the dirty bed sheet in her hands. It is covered in vomit and sweat.

女仆Ⅱ：她那样温和，长相秀丽，年纪还小却成熟稳重，待人亲切有礼……
Maid II: She's still young, but she's already such a perfect little lady. Gentle, polite, beautiful, kind ...

女仆Ⅱ：一个人要上那样多的课程，礼仪、文学、历史、绘画、声乐……甚至还需要在每个月份抽出两个夜晚，专门跟随夫人前往降灵会……
Maid II: She's got so many lessons to attend, bless her. Etiquette, literature, history, paintin', music, dance ... And on top of all that, she has to go to the seances with her mother twice a month ...

女仆Ⅱ：哎……多可怜啊……
Maid II: Poor girl ...

她轻而缓地叠起那张床单，像是包裹住一个难堪的纰漏。
She slowly folds up the bed sheet, as if to save Isolde the embarrassment.

女仆Ⅱ：她现在还没有开始晕厥，不大会彻底地陷入恍惚和疯狂，只是时常呕吐和眩晕……真希望她永远不至于变成伊文洁琳夫人那样……
Maid II: She don't faint often, and she ain't havin' any manic episodes like her mother does now. But she does vomit and feel dizzy at times ... Oh I do hope she don't end up like her mother ...

女仆Ⅰ：哎呀，好啦！那是小姐的事儿，不是我们的事儿！被单叠好了，我们赶紧走吧！
Maid I: Yes, yes, enough of the worryin'. It's none of our concern. You done with the sheets? Come on then.

女仆们抱着脏衣篮，从一处走廊中离去。
With the laundry baskets stowed under their arms, the maids walk out into the corridor.

伊索尔德：……
Isolde: ...

伊索尔德：……对，我记得上次我把它放在了这个抽屉里。啊，有了，在这儿。
Isolde: ... Yes, now I remember. I left it in this drawer. Ah, there it is.

在抽屉深处摸索着，年轻的小主人若有所思地停下了动作。
She fumbles in the dark until her fingers come across a familiar shape.

她看向手中，又看向昏暗不见光明的走廊，女仆们早不见踪影，她们热络的交谈却依然留在她耳边。
She looks at her palm, then into the dimly lit corridor. The maids are long gone by now, but their conversation still lingers in her ears.

伊索尔德：……父亲曾教育我——
Isolde: Father once told me ...

伊索尔德闭上双眼。
She shuts her eyes in calm determination.

伊索尔德：我们应当保持优雅，我们应当自我克制，我们应当热情亲切。
Isolde: Be graceful, be self-restrained, and be kind to others.

伊索尔德：我们应当心怀怜悯，我们应当帮助弱者。
Isolde: Be compassionate, especially towards the less privileged.

伊索尔德：我们……应当作为表率、见证与正确的范例，我们……
Isolde: We ... must be an example for our people, we—

西奥菲尔：我们应当拥有贵族真正的品德与尊严。
Theophil: We must be virtuous and dignified, as befits our status.

伊索尔德：……西奥菲尔，我从不知道今天你也在家。
Isolde: ... Theophil! I didn't know you were home today.

伊索尔德睁开眼，她年长两岁的兄弟站在一旁。
She opens her eyes. Her brother, two years her senior, is standing next to her.

西奥菲尔：我不打算停留太久的，只是母亲说你往这里来了，我就来找你。
Theophil: I won't be staying long. Mother told me you were here, so I came.

西奥菲尔：我们很久没见面了，我为你买了这个。
Theophil: It's been a while! I brought you this.

他一面说，一面从怀中抽出一个绒布盒子与一枚小怀表。
He pulls a little velvet box and a pocket watch from his jacket pocket.

西奥菲尔：盒子是海因里希托我转交的，里头有一枚胸针，这是他送给你的十五岁生日礼物。
Theophil: The box is from Heinrich. There's a brooch inside—for your fifteenth birthday.

伊索尔德：这枚怀表呢？
Isolde: And this watch?

西奥菲尔：这是我送给你的。
Theophil: That's from me.

伊索尔德：你已经送过我生日礼物了，西奥菲尔。
Isolde: But you already gave me a present on my birthday, Theophil.

西奥菲尔：不，这不是生日礼物。它能计时，也能定时，还有……
Theophil: No, it's not for your birthday. I just want you to have it. Look, it's not just a watch. It has a timer, and here ...

兄长按下开关，怀表翻盖打开。
He presses the little knob on the pocket watch, and, with a quiet click, it flips open.

西奥菲尔：你看，是白水晶，这也是一个护身符。它能安定精神，旁边还有一个小插口，里面装着能使用二十次左右的嗅盐。
Theophil: See? It's white crystal. It works like an amulet to help you stay calm. And here, see the slot? It can hold enough smelling salts for twenty uses.

西奥菲尔：你需要看时间预估课程，你也需要护身符和嗅盐。你会需要它的。
Theophil: You need a watch to keep track of the time in all those lessons, and the amulet and smelling salts will help you, too. It's perfect.

西奥菲尔将怀表放在妹妹的手心里，他顿了一顿，又重新开口。
He presses the watch into his sister's hand. There is a beat of silence. He speaks again.

西奥菲尔：最重要的是，我还得告诉你一个好消息，我租下了一间画室，那儿会给我更多灵感。
Theophil: I also have news for you: I'm renting a studio. It's amazing how much it's helped me gain inspiration!

西奥菲尔：当然，也更方便我的模特们留宿。我实在不忍让她们在寒风中独自返回破败的小屋。
Theophil: It also has space for the young models to stay the night when the weather becomes dreadful. It simply broke my heart to see them walking all the way back home in the rain.

西奥菲尔：一切都很好，只是我会回来得更少，我希望你和母亲一起坚持治疗，记得，我永远都在挂念着你，好吗？
Theophil: So, all is well with me, but I won't be returning home very frequently. I hope you'll continue your treatment along with Mother. Remember, I'm thinking of you all the time. You are my beloved little sister, after all.

伊索尔德：……父亲同意了吗？
Isolde: ... Did Father agree to this?

西奥菲尔：哦，当然！你大可以询问父亲，那地址甚至是他帮我选的呢！
Theophil: Of course! You can ask him if you like. He even helped pick the studio.

西奥菲尔回过头，招呼更远处的父亲，他开心地笑着，如同故事中春日的信使、游移不定的风神。
He turns around and waves to their father, a bright, beautiful smile on his face. For a moment, he looks like the herald of spring.

伊索尔德：……原来如此。这么一来，我也就放心你了，西奥菲尔。
Isolde: I see. Then I'm relieved. I wish only the best for you, Theophil.

春日信使在父亲的照拂下欢呼着，轻快地乘风而去。如同往日的他一样，从会客厅走向通往外界的门，踏入广大的世界中去了。
The spring's favored son leaves as briskly as the wind. He crosses the lounge, passes through the doors, and goes out into the boundless world.

迪塔斯多夫先生站在女儿身旁，将手放在她一侧的肩上，连连摇头。
Dittarsdorf stands next to his daughter, a hand on her shoulder. He shakes his head.

伊索尔德：对了，您刚刚在我家中的时候，有没有留意到过一扇门？
Isolde: By the way, when you were in the house just now, did you notice a door?

卡卡尼亚：一扇门？那是一扇什么样子的门？
Kakania: A door? What kind of door?

伊索尔德：只是一扇普通的门，再普通不过的门。
Isolde: It's a door. One of the most common types. No one would notice it was placed in such a big, magnificent mansion.

伊索尔德：我在十三岁生日的夜晚遇到了它。
Isolde: It appeared on the evening of my thirteenth birthday.

伊索尔德：那时我又一次吐在了我的床上，我无法消化蛋糕和烤好的鸡肉，它们太干了，我的胃又太疼了。
Isolde: I vomited on my bed that night. I couldn't digest all that cake and roast chicken. My stomach hurt.

伊索尔德：那些游灵弯下腰来凑近我，摆出滑稽的嘴脸，双手鼓动，无声地为我的呕吐喝彩。
Isolde: Those wandering spirits bent over me, making funny faces and silently clapping as I heaved up my dinner.

伊索尔德：楼下的宾客们等待着我，我必须换好新的礼服，再一次回到宴会厅中送别，感谢每一个人愿意前来庆祝我的成长。
Isolde: The guests downstairs were waiting for me. I had to get myself together to go back and thank everyone for celebrating my birthday with me.

伊索尔德：可我太累了，我已经几个月没有睡好觉了。
Isolde: But I was too tired. I hadn't had a good night's sleep in months.

伊索尔德：……也是这时候，我第一次想到了那个绝妙的好主意。
Isolde: And that was when I came up with the idea.

伊索尔德：我邀请了活跃的游灵来到我身上，无论它们情愿与不情愿。
Isolde: I invited the spirits to fall upon me, whether they were willing or not.

伊索尔德：善于逢迎的游灵，善于歌舞的游灵，善于行端坐正的游灵，善于撒娇卖乖的游灵，善于读写文学的游灵——它们开始协助我完成那些困难的任务。
Isolde: I utilized the spirits' proficiencies to do what I couldn't do alone. They helped me flatter my guests, behave well, sing and dance, read and write literature ... They aided me in everything.

伊索尔德：我可以休息了。
Isolde: And I was able to rest.

伊索尔德：我终于，可以休息了。
Isolde: I was finally able to rest.

伊索尔德：……
Isolde: ...

西奥菲尔的葬礼事宜已经落幕，大半仆人被允许告假返乡。宅邸的女主人再不见客，对外宣称自己需要安静的修养环境。
Theophil's funeral is over. More than half of the servants have been let go. Ms. Dittasdorf says she needs some time alone and refuses to receive any visitors.

伊索尔德穿过客厅，径直前往餐柜旁。
Isolde crosses the living room, walking straight to the sideboard.

伊索尔德：玛姬不在家里。没有人会替我喂养花儿与苹果卷。还有那数十只没有名字的小兔、夜莺与天鹅。
Isolde: Maggie isn't home, so there's no one to take care of Blume, Apfelstrudel, and the nightingale and swan ...

她拉开抽屉，将手伸入其中，捡出一个金属制的小盒，其中盛满砷粉。
She opens the drawer and takes out a metal box of arsenic powder.

毒药之中的毒药，由遥远的古罗马延绵至今的谋杀之王。
It has been the most deadly poison since the time of ancient Rome until today.

伊索尔德：可我得为它们负起责任，这是我应当去做的。
Isolde: They're my responsibility now.

伊索尔德：来，苹果卷、花儿，来吃这个……
Isolde: Here, Apfelstrudel, Blume, come have some ...

伊索尔德双手捧着小盒，轻巧地回身，向着窗边喝茶的小桌走去。
She carries the box over to the tea table by the window and places the box on it.

伊索尔德：我们应当保持优雅，我们应当自我克制，我们应当热情亲切。
Isolde: Be graceful, be self-restrained, and be kind to others.

茶壶倾斜，热茶流淌入茶杯。
She tilts the tea pot and watches the tea flow into the cup.

伊索尔德：我们应当心怀怜悯，我们应当帮助弱者。
Isolde: Be compassionate, especially towards the less privileged.

伊索尔德：没关系，别害怕。你们可以逃，你们有权利离开这里，离开这个废墟……
Isolde: There's nothing to be afraid of. You have the right to leave this ruined world ...

伊索尔德：我们应当作为表率……见证与正确的范例。
Isolde: We ... must be an example for our people.

伊索尔德：你在抽搐，天啊，别怕，我会为你按住身体。
Isolde: Oh dear, you're twitching ... It's alright. I'll hold you.

伊索尔德：我们应当拥有……
Isolde: We must ...

伊索尔德垂下眼睛，砷粉溶于茶汤，消失无踪。
Isolde drops her eyes. The arsenic powder dissolves in the hot tea as if it had never existed.

她端起茶杯，轻微低下头颅。
She raises the tea cup and slightly lowers her head.

伊索尔德：我们应当拥有贵族真正的品德与尊严，西奥菲尔。
Isolde: We must be virtuous and dignified, as befits our status, Theophil.

茶汤中的春风之神面容焦黑，悲伤地凝视着她。
In the tea cup, her charred brother gazes at her sorrowfully.

伊索尔德：啊……瞧瞧我，我多么大意……我忘记了我今日还有约定。
Isolde: Ah ... How careless I am. I almost forgot the date today.

伊索尔德抬起头，丢去茶杯，将春风之神的面孔泼洒在地面上。
She raises her head and throws the tea cup over her shoulder, splashing her brother's face on the floor.

她拿起衣架上的外套，向着门廊走去，口中念念有词。
She picks up her coat from the hanger and walks toward the porch, muttering to herself.

伊索尔德：我的新医生，我的卡卡尼亚……我的卡卡尼亚医生。
Isolde: My new doctor, my Kakania ... Dr. Kakania.

提早到达约会地点，这似乎算是一种礼仪。
It is good etiquette for a lady to arrive early to an appointment.

伊索尔德：不过没关系，我可以等待一段时间。我并不是一个缺乏耐心的人。
Isolde: That's alright. I can wait. I have patience.

伊索尔德：卡卡尼亚小……啊，不，或许我现在应该习惯叫她医生，卡卡尼亚医生。
Isolde: Ms. Kakania ... Oh, I suppose I should call her Dr. Kakania from now on.

伊索尔德：医生没有在这里悬挂诊疗室的门牌……啊，对了，她还没有行医执照，她不能这么做……
Isolde: There's no sign indicating that this is the consultation room ... Oh, right, she doesn't have a medical license.

伊索尔德：啊，所以她把门刷成了这种颜色，好方便患者分辨她的所在。这是一个很聪明的决定。
Isolde: Ah, so she's painted the door this color so that the patients can tell where she is. How very clever.

在等待之中，客人轻声呢喃。
As she waits, Isolde murmurs softly to herself.

伊索尔德：这个世界上有这样多的门。
Isolde: There are so many doors in this world.

伊索尔德：……医生是门吗？她是一扇什么样的门呢？
Isolde: Is Dr. Kakania a door, too? What kind of door would she be, I wonder?

伊索尔德：她会是绿色的吗，会有丝绸一般的光泽吗，会……
Isolde: Would it be green or shining like silk? Perhaps ...

另一道声音打断了呓语。
She is interrupted by a familiar voice.

卡卡尼亚：嗯？伊索尔德？
Kakania: Hmm? Isolde?

卡卡尼亚：哦——抱歉！我迟到了！
Kakania: Sorry, I'm late!

活跃的绿裙子摇晃着，卡卡尼亚快步小跑而来。
Kakania trots over to her, her lively green skirt swaying with each step.

卡卡尼亚：我有事耽误了一段时间……你知道的，我总是遇到麻烦，我发誓，我本来想提早回来等您的——毕竟您今天是第一次来，这可是建立医患关系的重要开端！
Kakania: I was delayed. You know what I'm like. I swear I'd planned to return earlier to ensure this didn't happen! This is our first consultation, after all! Oh, I so wanted to have a good start to our doctor-patient relationship!

卡卡尼亚：真对不起，您等很久了吗？
Kakania: I'm really, very sorry. Have you been waiting long?

卡卡尼亚：您站得累了吗？您之前还晕倒了一次，理论上来说不应该晒着太阳站这么久的……
Kakania: Are you tired? Oh, I shouldn't have kept you waiting under the sun ...

口中的话语不断，卡卡尼亚从腰侧掏出一枚系着绿色细带的金钥匙。

那与门上的金锁眼是一对，当然如此。她将其插入、扭转，打开了诊疗室的门。
A golden key to the golden lock on the door. She inserts it and turns it. The door opens.

伊索尔德：好，我就来。

There she is, reflected in the Vingler's Mirror, the daughter of a mirror seller. Klara. Dr. Kakania.
