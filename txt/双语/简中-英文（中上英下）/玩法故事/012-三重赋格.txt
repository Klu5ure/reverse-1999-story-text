01  疲惫主义 / Exhaustion

疲惫主义
Exhaustion

战壕
Trenches

火炮声终于停息，毒气也消散殆尽，士兵们正陆续从防空洞里钻出。
The cannon fire fades as the poison gas dissipates. They rise from the trenches, these men, not yet dead, but not quite alive.

他们挥开余烬和烟尘，再一次打量世界。
They brush away the cinders and smoke and look out upon the world once more.

弗兰兹：那边的新兵还没有回过神来。
Franz: That rookie's still in shock.

卡戎：……嗯？
Charon: Hmm ...?

弗兰兹：看来你也没有……到现在我也分辨不出你是反应迟钝，还是正在走神。
Franz: Looks like you haven't snapped out of it either ... Still can't tell if you're just slow or off in your own little world.

弗兰兹：千万注意脚下，摔进弹坑里得遭不小的罪。
Franz: Watch your step. Fall into a shell hole and you'll regret it fast.

弗兰兹：……不过我说这话好像显得有些多余，我们也不是第一次做战场清扫。
Franz: ... Not that you need the reminder. We've been through this palaver before.

喋喋不休的士兵掰掉战友识别牌的下半，然后审慎地把钢盔盖在了战友的脸上。
As he talks, the soldier snaps off the lower half of his comrade's dog tag before carefully lowering the helmet over his face.

卡戎：当毒气还在防空洞里蔓延，他就摘下了防毒面具。
Charon: He removed his mask, even with gas hanging heavy in the shelter.

弗兰兹：新兵最容易犯这样的错。
Franz: Typical rookie mistake.

弗兰兹：他们对安全和危险的判别，都依赖自己的感受。
Franz: Better not to trust your gut to tell you what's safe and what's not when you're so green.

弗兰兹：他们太希望该死的毒气早点散去，以至于欺骗了自己，所以他们面目全非地躺在了这里。
Franz: They want the cursed gas gone so badly that they lie to themselves and just pull the things off before it's gone. Now look at them—can hardly recognize the Frontschweine.

卡戎费了些时间看清他眼中的阴翳。
Charon takes a moment to study the shadow clouding his vision.

卡戎：你很难过。
Charon: Misery lies heavy on you.

弗兰兹：哈！难过是什么感觉？我要是知道，我就没法在这儿继续呆下去。
Franz: Ha! If I could still feel misery, do you think I'd be standing here right now?

卡戎：我理解，在战壕里生存，几乎所有的感受都会消失，最后只剩下忍耐。
Charon: ... In the trenches, feelings fade until only the will to endure remains.

卡戎：你想为他念祷词吗？
Charon: Will you say a prayer for him?

弗兰兹：那不是你该做的事情吗？
Franz: Isn't that your job?

卡戎：你知道我并没有信仰，但是如果念祷词可以让人们感到好受，那么我会去做。
Charon: There is no faith in me, but if a prayer can ease their pain, one will be offered.

弗兰兹：好吧，好吧，你去看看那边的倒霉蛋们，这小子和我是同乡，我来送他。
Franz: Alright, alright. You go check on the poor bastards over there. This kid's from my hometown. I'll take care of him.

士兵在他那位同乡面前站直，似乎是在竭力撇掉身上的那股痞气。
The soldier straightens up before his fallen townsman, as if trying to shake off his informal demeanor.

卡戎注视了他一会儿，随后转身，推动他那架用活动木板搭成的小车。
Charon studies him a moment longer, then turns and rolls his makeshift cart forward.

士兵Ⅰ：噢，卡戎，哪里有死者，哪里就有你。
Soldier I: Heh ... Charon, wherever death goes, you follow right behind.

士兵们摇晃着手里装有识别牌的袋子，向他简短地打了招呼。
The bags of dog tags in their hands jingle as the soldiers give him terse nods.

士兵Ⅱ：弗兰兹应该很伤心，躺着的那小子是他中学老师的孩子，他一直很照顾那小子。
Soldier II: Franz must be cut up. That boy ... he was his teacher's kid—like a younger brother to him.

士兵Ⅰ：他天天给那群新兵上课，教他们分辨轻重炮弹的嗡鸣，教他们掐准手榴弹的时间。
Soldier I: He trained those rookies every damn day. Taught them how to spot the whistle of a shell, how to time a grenade to the second ...

卡戎：在恐惧面前，这些知识会被忘得一干二净。
Charon: When fear knocks on the door ... training often disappears.

士兵Ⅰ：总之，他尽力了，我们都尽力了。
Soldier I: Well, he did his best. That's all any of us can do.

卡戎没再答话，遗体的识别牌已被掰下，他检查了那截摇摇欲坠的手臂。
Charon says no more. With the tag removed, he turns to examine the fallen soldier's barely attached arm.

士兵们像往常那样帮他扶起遗体的头脚，然后一起一落，小车发出嘎吱声。
The soldiers move the body in the same way they always do, by the head and feet, up then down. The cart creaks beneath the weight.

士兵Ⅱ：……该死，又是“告别时间”。
Soldier II: Shoot ... another goodbye.

士兵们骂骂咧咧，但卡戎的目光却放在不远处的泥泞上。
The soldiers curse and grumble, but Charon's gaze remains fixed on the patch of mud ahead.

卷曲的黑白三角，它的下半部分被淹没在泥水之中，但依然鲜明得足够引起注意。
A crumpled triangle of black and white sticks out from the mud.

它在风中颤颤巍巍地抖动，犹如等待撷取的花瓣。
It quivers in the wind, like a fallen petal.

士兵Ⅰ：卡戎？
Soldier I: Charon?

士兵Ⅱ：确实很难分清他是走神还是反应慢。
Soldier II: I honestly still can't tell if he's daydreaming or just slow in the head.

卡戎走上前，轻轻地抽出了那张相片。
Charon steps forward and slowly pulls it from the mud.

士兵Ⅱ：……等等，这是什么？
Soldier II: Hang on, what's this?

在泥泞、硝烟、火炮、苦痛和死亡之外的另一个世界。
A world apart from all this—the mud, the smoke, the shelling, the suffering, and death.

一个女性将她的头发梳得一丝不苟，怀抱一把新鲜的矢车菊。
A woman with neatly parted hair holds a bouquet of fresh cornflowers in her arms.

用柔情暗昧的面容，对着这个世界微笑。
She smiles a tender smile, an unspoken longing hidden somewhere behind her eyes.

士兵Ⅱ：噢！这是谁呀。
Soldier II: Oh? Who's this, then?

士兵Ⅰ：应该是谁的妻子，呃，也可能是妹妹、母亲？
Soldier I: I bet she's his wife ... or, uh, maybe his sister? His mother?

卡戎：那么她的丈夫、哥哥或者儿子在哪儿？
Charon: Then where is her husband, her brother, or her son?

卡戎：你们有可能知道这张相片的主人吗？
Charon: Might any of you know who this photograph once belonged to?

士兵Ⅰ：至少我们连没有这号人，我可以很肯定地这样说。
Soldier I: I can tell you right now, he's not from our company.

卡戎：我明白了，我会到处问问。
Charon: Then those elsewhere must be asked.

卡戎打开随身的笔记本，将那张相片夹了进去。
Charon opens his notebook and slides the photograph between its pages.

不远处的人群突然骚动，呼喝声迭起交错。
Unrest ripples through the nearby crowd.

弗兰兹：卡戎！来帮帮忙！
Franz: Charon! Get over here and give us a hand!

弗兰兹：这里有个没死的伤兵，担架和人手都不够，你送他去野战医院吧！
Franz: We've got a wounded man and no way to move him. We need you, Charon! Get him to the field hospital!

士兵拍了拍卡戎的肩膀，后者回过头来，短暂地消化了弗兰兹的呼唤。
A soldier pats Charon on the shoulder. He turns, quietly following Franz's summons.

卡戎：好，我来送他。
Charon: Jawohl. He will be delivered.

战地医院
Field Hospital

从卡戎踏进医院开始，一道视线就落在他身上。
The moment Charon crosses the field hospital's threshold, a pair of eyes locks on him.

直到他运送来的伤兵被护士们接管，视线的主人才走上前来与他搭话。
Only once the nurses take the wounded soldier does the watcher step forward to speak.

沃尔克中校：卡戎，艾伯哈特的士官，是吧？
Lieutenant Colonel Wolker: You're Charon, Eberhard's officer. Is that right?

卡戎：是的，长官。
Charon: Yes, sir.

沃尔克中校：你倒是个蛮有意思的家伙。
Lieutenant Colonel Wolker: You're a curious sort, aren't you?

沃尔克中校：一个死而复生的士兵，拒绝了宣传部的要求，抛弃了升衔获勋的机会。
Lieutenant Colonel Wolker: A man returns from death itself, then refuses honors and turns down the press bureau.

沃尔克中校：你到前线来，就为了收殓士兵和运送伤员。
Lieutenant Colonel Wolker: Did you come to the front just to ferry the wounded and bury the dead?

卡戎：……
Charon: ...

沃尔克中校：看来你不善言辞，拒绝宣传部倒也合情合理。
Lieutenant Colonel Wolker: Not the talkative type, I see. Little wonder you turned down the press.

卡戎：我以为您这样的长官，会对我拒绝宣传部的事情颇有微词。
Charon: Officers of your rank generally disapprove of reluctance to work with the press bureau.

沃尔克中校：哈哈，没那回事。
Lieutenant Colonel Wolker: Hah! Not at all.

沃尔克中校：人们总在谈论英雄主义和钢铁青年，高中生们因为这些口号涌向了战场。
Lieutenant Colonel Wolker: Stories of heroism, steel-hearted youths—this is the stuff that sends schoolboys off to die.

沃尔克中校：他们以为这是一次奥德修斯之旅，最后却踏进一篇与想象背道而驰的悲剧。
Lieutenant Colonel Wolker: They marched off, grand images of Odysseus's voyage in their minds, only to find themselves cast in a tale far crueler than they could ever have conceived.

沃尔克中校：而你实行的是自己的英雄主义，即使这和“我们”的意志无关。
Lieutenant Colonel Wolker: You follow your own form of heroism, regardless of what "we" stand for.

沃尔克中校：可能其他人会认为你是个怪人，但我欣赏你，也希望自己能理解你。
Lieutenant Colonel Wolker: Some might call you strange, but me—I respect you. I'd like to understand you.

卡戎：……不，中校，我想您误会了，我与任何主义都没有关系。
Charon: ... You are mistaken, Herr Oberstleutnant. There is no ideology behind the actions of the dead.

卡戎：我只是对这一切都感到疲惫而已。
Charon: Only exhaustion. That is all.

片刻的沉默。中校的目光放得很远，似乎落在不具名的彼方。
A moment passes in silence. The lieutenant colonel's gaze stretches outward, fixed on some unseen, unnamed horizon.

沃尔克中校：你很诚实。
Lieutenant Colonel Wolker: You're an honest man.

卡戎：我只是认为我没有说谎的必要。
Charon: Lying simply serves me no purpose.

沃尔克中校：四天之后，这所野战医院就会撤到后方，因为这里也很可能变成前线。
Lieutenant Colonel Wolker: Four days from now, this field hospital will be packed up and sent to the rear. This is likely to become the front line.

沃尔克中校：到时候，会有许多伤员和遗体被留下。
Lieutenant Colonel Wolker: When that time comes, many dead and wounded will be left behind.

卡戎：我会体面地安葬他们。
Charon: They will be laid to rest with dignity.

沃尔克中校：看来你对战争中的无可奈何已经习以为常，即使是这种非人道的抛弃。
Lieutenant Colonel Wolker: You've grown accustomed to the helplessness of war, it seems—even to this kind of cruel abandonment.

卡戎：我的愤怒或抗议都无足轻重，我只会做好那些我能做到的事情。
Charon: There are no words, neither angered or reasoned, that can be said to change things.

短暂的谈话拉长了他们身上的滞重，沉默过去之后，中校向他告了别。
Though brief, the conversation only adds to the burden weighing heavily on both their shoulders. After a pause, the lieutenant colonel quietly moves to depart.

沃尔克中校：那么，我要去做我该做的事情了——我们应该还会见面的。
Lieutenant Colonel Wolker: I have duties to attend to. I believe we'll cross paths again.

卡戎：再见，中校。
Charon: Farewell, Herr Oberstleutnant.

如同齿轮转动，卡戎明确自己的下一步是取回自己十分钟前放下的笔记本。
Like a gear locking into place, Charon moves to his next task: retrieving the notebook he set down just ten minutes earlier.

他抬步转弯，回到刚刚将士兵转移上担架的位置。
With a step and a turn, he retraces his path to where the soldier was lifted onto the stretcher.

有限的视野中，他的笔记被人捧在手里，书页中的蝴蝶纹样随同她的双手微微颤抖。
In his narrow field of view, he catches sight of his notebook cradled in a pair of trembling hands.

卡戎：……！
Charon: ...!

她看过来，没有丝毫查看他人私人物品的歉意。
Its holder looks up. Her expression holds no hint of remorse for handling something so personal.

？？？：这是你的笔记本？
???: Is this your notebook?

卡戎：……是的。
Charon: ... It is.

？？？：很漂亮的蝴蝶纹样，你喜欢蝴蝶标本吗？
???: This butterfly pattern is lovely. Do you collect butterflies?

卡戎：……
Charon: ...

面对卡戎的沉默，女性似乎不以为意。
Charon says nothing, but the woman doesn't seem to mind.

？？？：我有两位认识了将近二十年的朋友——这个数字意味着我们从记事开始就认识了。
???: I've got these two friends I've known for almost twenty years—long enough that we can't even remember when we first met.

？？？：我和哥哥是孤儿，所以这两位朋友就像我的家人。
???: We lost our parents young, my brother and I, so those friends were the closest thing we had to family.

？？？：一位是我的未婚夫瓦尔特，他死在战场上。
???: One of them became my fiancé—Walter was his name. He died on the battlefield.

？？？：另一位喜欢收集蝴蝶标本，我和瓦尔特总是坐在他家的楼梯下面等他。
???: The other loved collecting butterflies. Walter and I used to sit under the stairs at his house waiting for him to show us.

？？？：他会拿来他的标本，神采飞扬地向我们介绍——那是他少有的，神采飞扬。
???: He'd bring out his specimens, face lit up like anything as he explained them to us. Those were the only times he seemed truly happy.

？？？：据我所知，我的这位朋友也已经死在了战场上，但有些可笑的流言，说他复活了。
???: I'd been sure he died out there too ... but then I heard these ridiculous rumors that he'd come back to life.

帕蒂塔：她蓝色的眼睛睨着他，像月光堆积在湖底。
Partita: She glances his way, her eyes a piercing blue.

？？？：我当时不信，但现在似乎我必须相信。
???: I didn't believe it at the time. But now ... I suppose I have no choice.

卡戎：……帕蒂塔。
Charon: ... Partita.

帕蒂塔：保罗，你为什么不回家？
Partita: Paul ... why didn't you come home?

02  来自过去 / From the Past

来自过去
From the Past

帕蒂塔：你为什么不愿意回答我的问题？
Partita: Why won't you answer me?

卡戎：我只是还在理解这个问题。
Charon: I ... am still trying to understand the question.

帕蒂塔：怎么？我成为战地护士让你非常惊讶？以至于你连句完整的话都说不出来？
Partita: What? Are you so shocked I became a field nurse? Can't even spit out a proper sentence?

卡戎：抱歉，我的确很惊讶，我已经很久没有你和你哥哥的消息了。
Charon: Apologies ... it must be shock. It has been so long since ... I ... heard news of you or your brother.

帕蒂塔：我哥？他的右腿被炸伤，移送到后方医院，然后死在了我面前。
Partita: My brother? His leg was torn up by a shell. They sent him to the rear hospital. He died right in front of me.

卡戎：……我很抱歉。
Charon: I ... I'm very sorry.

帕蒂塔：没什么好抱歉的，你也是个士兵。
Partita: No need to apologize. You're a soldier too.

卡戎：你为什么要来前线？
Charon: Why did you come to the front?

帕蒂塔：我想知道瓦尔特是怎么死的。
Partita: I want to know how Walter died.

卡戎：……帕蒂塔，在战场上寻找一个人的死因是毫无意义的。
Charon: ... Searching for the cause of a man's death on the battlefield is meaningless.

帕蒂塔：一个人的死因不该毫无意义。
Partita: No one should die without a reason.

女性的发言使得卡戎停滞半晌，他艰难地组织着语言。
Her words leave Charon silent. For a long moment, he struggles to find the right words.

卡戎：虽然我不知道你来了多久，但你的确是一名战地护士。
Charon: How long you've spent here is not known to me, but it is clear you're a field nurse.

卡戎：你应该知道，在火炮、机枪和坦克面前，人的生命脆弱且渺小，以至于不值一提。
Charon: You ought to know that when faced with artillery, machine guns, and tanks, a man's life is brief and fragile. There is little worth in it.

卡戎：瓦尔特死于一枚炮弹，但还有许多人甚至只能被标记上失踪。
Charon: Walter was struck by a shell—to know that much is a small mercy many are denied.

卡戎：所以我更愿意相信你刚刚对我说的“寻找瓦尔特死因”只是一种敷衍，它不会有答案。
Charon: The words you spoke of Walter's death ... are merely a deflection. There was never meant to be an answer.

帕蒂塔：这不是一种敷衍，你知道我从不敷衍你。
Partita: That's not true. You know I'm not that kind of person.

帕蒂塔：总是敷衍我和瓦尔特，总是回避问题，总是装作若无其事的人是你。
Partita: You're the one who always brushed us off, dodged the truth, pretended nothing was wrong ...

卡戎：你在生气。
Charon: You're angry.

帕蒂塔：你为什么不回家？
Partita: Why didn't you come home?

无法回答的问题也必须被找到一个答案，即使是临时的。
In such situations, even if there is no true answer, an answer must be found. Anything will do.

卡戎：我不知道现在的我该怎么回家。
Charon: I ... no longer know how.

帕蒂塔：我们需要你，不管是过去的你还是现在的你。
Partita: We need you, Paul. Whether you're the man we knew or whoever it is you are now.

帕蒂塔：你为什么不回家看看你的妈妈和姐姐？
Partita: Why won't you come home? Why won't you see your mother and sister?

帕蒂塔：看看已经失去瓦尔特的我们？
Partita: Why didn't you come to see us after we lost Walter?

卡戎：……帕蒂塔，我真的不是不想回答你。
Charon: ... Partita, it is not that there is no desire to give you an answer.

帕蒂塔：那么，瓦尔特死在你面前，你来告诉我，是什么样的炮击？
Partita: He died right in front of you. So tell me ... what kind of artillery did it?

卡戎：你看过我的笔记本了，你应该已经知道……
Charon: You have read the notebook. You already know—

帕蒂塔：那上面只有瓦尔特死去的日期。
Partita: No, I don't. There's only the date he died in there.

卡戎：那就是一次炮击。
Charon: Shellfire. That's all.

帕蒂塔：你为什么要撕掉后半页？
Partita: I saw the ripped pages. Why did you tear out the rest of it?

卡戎：……
Charon: ...

她的眼神黯淡下去，随后发出一声短促的冷笑。
The light fades from her eyes. Then, a short, bitter laugh escapes her.

帕蒂塔：我真是受够了，保罗。
Partita: I've had enough, Paul.

帕蒂塔：我看着哥哥死在我面前，他是自杀的。
Partita: I watched my brother take his own life right in front of me.

卡戎：……为什么？
Charon: ... Why?

卡戎无法想象那个独自抚养妹妹长大的人会自杀。
Charon cannot fathom that a man who raised his sister would choose to take his own life.

帕蒂塔的目光更像一种轻蔑，但被轻蔑对待的或许是她自己。
Partita's eyes hold a bitter edge, yet perhaps it's herself she cannot forgive.

帕蒂塔：伤口感染后的他就像在凌迟重辟的疼痛里，等待死刑的那一次落刀。
Partita: Once the wound got infected, it was like torture—he was just waiting for it to end.

帕蒂塔：我没能留下他。
Partita: I couldn't save him.

卡戎：这不是你的错，我们都尽力了。
Charon: All have done what they could. It wasn't your fault.

帕蒂塔：我哥死后一周，你写信说瓦尔特死了，那封信是寄到他家里的，我一路跑着去。
Partita: A week after my brother died, you sent the letter saying Walter was gone. You mailed it to his house. I ran all the way there.

帕蒂塔：我希望我不是最后一个看见你痛苦灵魂的人。
Partita: I hope I'm not the last person to see the pain inside you.

帕蒂塔：我希望你可以找到真正的自己。
Partita: I hope you one day find who you truly are.

我没有什么自己。他想要这样回答她，却如鲠在喉。
"There is no 'me' left to find." That is what he wishes he could say, but the words fail to materialize.

她站了起来，向士兵们走去。
She stands up and walks toward the soldiers.

卡戎：帕蒂塔——
Charon: Partita—

她没有回头。
She doesn't look back.

卡戎看见正在拿起枪的弗兰兹，后者意识到他的目光，僵硬而冷漠地扭过了头。
Charon watches as Franz picks up his rifle. Noticing his stare, Franz turns away, as stiff and cold as stone.

有什么东西从卡戎不存在的五脏中出现，然后涌向了他的四肢百骸。
Something stirs in the space where his innards should be and spreads through every inch of him.

它的每一次牵动都令卡戎不适，但他依然只是看着弗兰兹将子弹上膛。
Each throbbing wave of it racks Charon with unease, but all he can do is watch as Franz loads the bullet.

弗兰兹的面孔变得模糊而又陌生，似乎他在用麻木的士兵面孔让自己继续动作。
Franz's expression is blurred, distant, unfamiliar—as though he's hiding behind a soldier's mask to execute his duty.

它消弭殆尽了。
And just like that—it's done.

弗兰兹不再看卡戎，他匆匆走入士兵之中，随他们鱼贯上车。
Franz doesn't even glance at Charon. He simply slips into the line of soldiers and boards the transport.

车群发出启动的轰鸣，碾过焦土与将死的草植，一路绝尘而去。
The convoy roars to life, grinding over scorched ground and dying weeds.

世界又是寂静的了。
Silence returns.

只有寂静的生命在等待他，等待一抔抔的沙土，将他们葬入土地。
Only the dead remain, awaiting his hand to bury them beneath the earth.

法比安，帕蒂塔，还有无数死在战场上，年轻而饱受折磨的面孔。
Fabien, Partita, countless others—the young, tortured faces of war.

他独自向他们走去，履行他最后的，也是唯一的职责。
He walks alone to meet them, to carry out his last and only duty.

他拾起铲子，扬起尘土。
He raises the shovel and plunges it into the earth.
